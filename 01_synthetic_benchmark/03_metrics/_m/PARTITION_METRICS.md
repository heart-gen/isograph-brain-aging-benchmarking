## 1. Why these metrics

The published `module_recovery_score` is a best-match Jaccard: each planted module is
credited with its single best-matching predicted module. That is a *recall-like*
quantity, and it is blind to over-partitioning — a method that shatters one planted
module into ten predicted fragments still scores well as long as one fragment matches.
The reviewer's point is that a metric with this blind spot cannot by itself support a
claim of superior module recovery.

The metrics added here close that gap:

| metric | what it charges for |
|---|---|
| `ari_planted` | **primary.** Whole-partition agreement over planted genes; penalises both splitting and merging |
| `ami_planted` | robustness to ARI's chance-correction assumptions |
| `homogeneity_planted` | are predicted modules pure? (low = **merging**) |
| `completeness_planted` | are planted modules kept together? (low = **fragmentation**) |
| `v_measure_planted` | harmonic mean of the two |
| `ari_assigned` / `ami_assigned` | coverage-conditional: restricted to planted genes the method actually assigned |
| `ari_universe` / `ami_universe` | background genes treated as one extra truth class |
| `ari_planted_blob` | sensitivity to the unassigned-gene convention |
| `frac_planted_assigned` | fraction of planted genes given any module |
| `module_recovery_{null_mean,excess,z,perm_p}` | item 4: recovery against a module-count-preserving null |

**Reproduction check.** The new code recomputes best-match Jaccard from the stored
partitions and reproduces the published `module_recovery_score` on every run:
`max |Δ| = 2.2e-16` over 12,394 runs. The partition metrics are therefore an addition
to the published metric, not a reinterpretation of it.

**Multiplicity.** `stats/summarize.py` assigns every metric a `metric_family`
(`core` / `partition`) and `stats/hypothesis_tests.py` runs BH *within* family
(core n=514, partition n=796). Enlarging a single shared family would have shifted
the already-reported `p_adj` values for the core metrics; the pooled correction is
retained as `p_adj_all_metrics` for anyone who wants it. 1,307 of 1,310 paired
bootstrap CIs contain their mean difference; the 3 exceptions are
`negative_control_noise` × `isograph_spearman_leiden`, where only one paired dataset
exists (`n_pairs = 1`) so the bootstrap and the Wilcoxon are both degenerate — those
rows carry NaN p and NaN CI and are excluded from every significance count.

## 2. Item 1 headline: the switch advantage survives ARI

Paired by simulation, IsoGraph VAE minus WGCNA, bootstrap 95% CI on the mean
difference, BH within the partition family:

| scenario | n | Δ ARI | 95% CI | FDR | Cliff's δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| idealized_switching | 360 | **+0.350** | [0.345, 0.354] | <1e-4 | 1.00 |
| noise_stress | 320 | **+0.337** | [0.334, 0.340] | <1e-4 | 1.00 |
| non_switching_background | 80 | **+0.278** | [0.214, 0.333] | <1e-4 | 0.82 |
| feature_space_interactions | 300 | **+0.246** | [0.226, 0.265] | <1e-4 | 0.42 |
| unequal_isoform_abundance | 80 | **+0.218** | [0.154, 0.281] | 2e-4 | 0.63 |

The advantage does not merely survive the fragmentation-sensitive metric, it **widens**
under it: on `idealized_switching` the recovery-score gap is +0.37 while the ARI gap is
+0.35. Every one of the 360 paired simulations favours IsoGraph — that claim rests on the
paired statistics (Wilcoxon W = 0, rank-biserial = 1.000), not on Cliff's δ, which is
0.99985 here because it ranks all cross-pairs of the two unpaired samples rather than the
within-simulation differences.
The mechanism is visible in the components: WGCNA's `homogeneity_planted` is ≈1.0
across these scenarios while its `completeness_planted` caps at ≈0.76. Its predicted
modules are pure but each planted module is split across several of them. That is the
fragmentation signature the reviewer asked us to measure rather than assert, and it is
exactly what best-match Jaccard was hiding.

## 2a. The same result resolved by sweep point, not averaged over the sweep

Every number in §2 is a scenario-level summary, and each scenario is a *sweep* over a
difficulty parameter. That makes both the paired difference and the per-method median
partly a function of how many easy points the sweep happens to contain, which is a design
choice rather than a property of the methods. `feature_space_interactions` is the clearest
case: IsoGraph's ARI runs from 1.000 at `interaction_strength <= 0.5` to 0.019 at
`interaction_strength >= 2` with `interaction_fraction = 0.75`, and the scenario median of
0.973 reports neither end. 120 of that scenario's 300 runs sit at strength 0 or 0.5, where
the task is trivial.

Reported instead by sweep point, from
`python -m isograph_benchmark.stats.sweep_breakdown`
(`01_synthetic_benchmark/03_metrics/_h/step_4_sweep_breakdown.sh`), which writes
`synthetic_sweep_breakdown.parquet` (per-point means/medians + bootstrap CIs),
`synthetic_sweep_range.parquet` (best/worst point per method), `synthetic_sweep_paired.parquet`
(the paired contrast computed *within* each point) and the rendered `SWEEP_BREAKDOWN.md`.

`metrics_ari_planted`, means over 20 replicates per cell:

| interaction_fraction \ strength | 0.0 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 1.000 / 0.660 | 1.000 / 0.655 | 0.949 / 0.515 | 0.521 / 0.365 | 0.541 / 0.358 |
| 0.50 | 1.000 / 0.660 | 1.000 / 0.654 | 0.888 / 0.416 | 0.150 / 0.116 | 0.152 / 0.107 |
| 0.75 | 1.000 / 0.660 | 1.000 / 0.658 | 0.816 / 0.523 | 0.019 / 0.016 | 0.020 / 0.009 |

(IsoGraph VAE / WGCNA.) `unequal_isoform_abundance` by `abundance_imbalance`: 1.000/0.654,
1.000/0.648, 0.993/0.650, **0.280/0.449**. `non_switching_background` by
`switching_fraction`: 0.657/0.577, 0.986/0.644, 1.000/0.660, 1.000/0.652.

**The comparative claim survives this resolution; the absolute one does not.** Across the
51 sweep points of the five headline scenarios, the paired within-point contrast is
`method_better` at **48**, `indistinguishable` at **3**, and `ref_better` at **none**. BH is
applied across all 103 sweep points of every scenario for this metric, so these are
corrected within their own multiplicity rather than borrowing §2's.

The three undemonstrated points are each the hardest point of their scenario:

| scenario | sweep point | n | Δ ARI | 95% CI | BH q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unequal_isoform_abundance | imbalance 25 | 20 | −0.169 | [−0.325, +0.002] | 0.110 |
| non_switching_background | switching_fraction 0.05 | 20 | +0.080 | [−0.141, +0.281] | 0.596 |
| feature_space_interactions | fraction 0.75, strength 2 | 20 | +0.004 | [−0.003, +0.010] | 0.179 |

Three things this table must not be made to say. It is **not** a reversal: only
`unequal_isoform_abundance` has a point estimate favouring WGCNA, and its CI contains
zero, so the honest statement is that the advantage is not demonstrated there, not that it
is absent or inverted. It is **not** an equivalence claim either — `indistinguishable`
here means the CI spans zero at n = 20, which at the `non_switching_background` point is
compatible with anything from −0.14 to +0.28. And the two `feature_space_interactions` and
`non_switching_background` points are ones where *both* methods have collapsed onto the
same near-zero floor (0.019 vs 0.016; 0.657 vs 0.577), so what disappears is the
separation between them, not IsoGraph's performance alone.

The manuscript-facing consequence is that the scenario-level medians should be read as
sweep summaries and not as robustness statements. The dose-response is the stronger result
anyway: it locates where the method breaks — `interaction_strength >= 2`,
`abundance_imbalance >= 25`, `switching_fraction <= 0.05` — instead of averaging across the
break point. Whenever a scenario-level figure is quoted, the sweep range from
`synthetic_sweep_range.parquet` should be quoted with it.

## 3. Where IsoGraph loses, and why it matters which kind of loss it is

IsoGraph VAE loses to WGCNA under ARI on four confound scenarios. The
coverage-conditional metric splits these into two mechanisms that need different
language in the manuscript.

| scenario | ari_planted | → ari_assigned | coverage | homogeneity | WGCNA ari_assigned | mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rna_degradation_coupled | 0.068 | 0.642 | 0.27 | 0.99 | 0.873 | **coverage** |
| abundance_switch_mixed | 0.201 | 0.605 | 0.66 | 1.00 | 0.719 | **coverage** |
| cell_composition | 0.185 | 0.188 | 1.00 | **0.44** | 0.515 | **merging** |
| library_depth | 0.116 | 0.117 | 1.00 | **0.38** | 0.505 | **merging** |

- **Coverage losses** (top two): IsoGraph declines to assign 73% / 34% of planted genes,
  but everything it does assign is grouped correctly (homogeneity ≈ 1.0). This is
  conservatism under `min_module_size`, not mis-grouping. Conditioning on coverage
  recovers most of the gap.
- **Merging losses** (bottom two): coverage is complete, so the low ARI is real —
  genes from different planted modules are being placed in the same predicted module
  when a global sample-level confound (depth, composition) dominates the correlation
  structure.

Both are honest limitations and both are stated as such. They are not the same
limitation and should not be reported with the same sentence.

### Both mechanisms have a method-side fix, and each fixes only one

Paired on identical simulations (n=80 per scenario):

| scenario | fix | ari_planted | mean Δ | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cell_composition | `isograph_vae_residual` | 0.185 → **0.643** | +0.334 | [0.269, 0.398] | 3.3e-11 |
| library_depth | `isograph_vae_residual` | 0.116 → **0.647** | +0.356 | [0.295, 0.416] | 8.0e-12 |
| batch_effects | `isograph_vae_residual` | 0.627 → 0.646 | +0.141 | [0.092, 0.194] | 1.0e-6 |
| rna_degradation_coupled | `isograph_vae_multiplex` | 0.068 → **0.657** | +0.584 | [0.580, 0.588] | 7.8e-15 |
| rna_degradation_coupled | `isograph_vae_residual` | 0.068 → 0.069 | +0.000 | [−0.001, 0.002] | 0.97 |

Residualization repairs **merging** and nothing else: `homogeneity_planted` goes
0.44 → 1.00 (`cell_composition`) and 0.38 → 1.00 (`library_depth`), and both variants
now *beat* WGCNA on the same simulations (+0.112 [0.101, 0.160] and +0.129
[0.105, 0.163]). It does not touch `rna_degradation_coupled`, because you cannot
residualize your way to genes the method never assigned.

The abundance-channel variant repairs **coverage**: on `rna_degradation_coupled`
`frac_planted_assigned` goes 0.27 → 0.96 and the deficit against WGCNA shrinks from
−0.681 [−0.725, −0.632] (δ = −0.98) to −0.097 [−0.142, −0.048] (δ = −0.60).
`isograph_vae_reliability` behaves identically (0.655). This is a substantial recovery
but **not parity** — the residual −0.097 gap survives FDR (p_adj = 2.8e-3), so the
correct phrasing is "largely recovered", not "recovered".

**Is residualization free when there is no confound? Yes, within a bound — closed by a
separate ablation.** Residualizing removes real signal whenever signal and covariate are
correlated, so "repairs the confound losses" needs a cost control. That control could not
be run from this grid: the cached unconfounded datasets predate the
`RIN`/`neuron_frac`/`batch`/`library_size` columns, so `build_design_matrix` receives
nothing and `isograph_vae_residual` silently reduces to `isograph_vae` — the ablation would
have reported "costs nothing" because it never ran. Repairing those sample tables is also
not possible: 1,204 cached datasets, including every core switch scenario, no longer
regenerate from the current generator (`01_synthetic_benchmark/01_synthetic/_m/SAMPLE_TABLE_REFRESH.md`).

It was therefore run as a **paired within-dataset contrast on 100 fresh, generator-pinned
datasets** across the five unconfounded scenarios, 20 seeds each
(`01_synthetic_benchmark/03_metrics/_m/RESIDUAL_COST.md`). Both arms see byte-identical input; only the
residualization flag differs. Mean ARI change **+0.0039, 95% CI +0.0002 to +0.0079, BH
q = 0.064** across the five metrics — **no cost detectable**. The point estimate is slightly
positive, consistent with library depth being a genuine nuisance axis even where no confound
was planted (`sample_depth` is a lognormal σ = 0.25 draw that scales `expected_total`), but
nothing survives multiple testing and this must not be reported as a benefit.

Two limits on that clause. It covers **library depth only** — `RIN`, `neuron_frac` and
`batch` are constants in unconfounded scenarios and `build_design_matrix` drops them — so it
is not a statement about residualization in general. And it is an **equivalence bounded by
the CI** (|Δ ARI| ≤ 0.008), not a proof of exactly zero effect.

**Still open.** The multiplex arm has been run only on the switch scenarios and has no
equivalent cost control.

**Reproducibility of the synthetic benchmark is by archive.** All benchmark datasets are
deposited alongside the manuscript. This is load-bearing rather than incidental: 1,204 of
the cached datasets — every core switch scenario — no longer regenerate from the current
generator, because the RNG stream shifted across generator commits
(`01_synthetic_benchmark/01_synthetic/_m/SAMPLE_TABLE_REFRESH.md`). The *design* is unchanged (identical
gene sets, module counts and module-size distributions); what differs is the random
realization, so the cached data is a valid draw from the documented process that current
code does not reproduce. Regenerating would replace the data behind every number in this
document with a different draw, so the datasets are retained as archived artifacts and the
archive — not the generator — is the reproducibility guarantee. Datasets created after
2026-08-14 additionally carry a generator pin (git commit + `synthetic_data.py` hash, e.g.
`01_synthetic_benchmark/00_design/_m/residual_cost_generator_pin.json`) so future drift is a loud
mismatch rather than a silent one.

## 4. Item 4: recovery calibrated against a module-count-preserving null

Best-match Jaccard rewards module count: a method emitting many small modules earns
matches by chance. For each run the planted-module assignment is permuted 1,000 times
holding the *predicted* partition's module sizes fixed, giving `null_mean`, `excess`,
`z` and `perm_p`.

**The acceptance gate passes exactly as predicted.** On `negative_control_noise`, where
nothing structured is planted:

| method | recovery | null mean | z | perm P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wgcna_gene | 0.500 | 0.441 | 0.47 | 0.746 |
| isograph_spearman_leiden | 0.000 | 0.487 | 0.29 | 0.920 |
| isograph_vae | 0.003 | 0.003 | −0.18 | 1.000 |
| isograph_vae_multiplex | 0.002 | 0.002 | 1.00 | 1.000 |

WGCNA's raw recovery score of **0.500 on pure noise** looks like half-perfect recovery
and is in fact entirely explained by its module-count structure (P = 0.75). On genuinely
planted scenarios the same statistic gives z = 13–58 at perm P = 0.005 (the 1,000-permutation
floor) for every method that recovers anything. The null is what makes a recovery score
interpretable, and it is now reported alongside the raw score everywhere.

## 5. Caveats that must travel with these numbers

1. **`negative_control_noise` has one planted module.** ARI, homogeneity, completeness
   and V-measure are all vacuously 1.0 when the truth partition has a single class, so
   `partition_metrics.py` suppresses them there (`truth["gene_id"].nunique() < 2`) and
   fig1 panel B renders that facet as "undefined (1 planted module)". The fragmentation
   diagnosis for that scenario comes from the item-4 null and the raw module count
   instead. An earlier draft of the backfill reported ARI = 1.000 for every method in
   this scenario; that number was an artifact and does not appear in any output.
2. **`ari_universe` ≈ 0.002–0.011 everywhere and is not a discriminator.** Treating all
   background genes as one additional truth class means that class dwarfs the eight
   planted ones, and no method recovers unstructured background as a coherent group.
   It is reported as a diagnostic only; do not read the near-zero values as a method
   failure.
3. **Cross-metric comparisons are within-family FDR.** A `p_adj` from the partition
   family is not directly comparable to one from the core family. Use
   `p_adj_all_metrics` if a single pooled statement is needed.
4. **Medians and paired means can disagree.** In `batch_effects` the residual arm's
   median ARI moves 0.627 → 0.646 while the paired mean difference is +0.141: the gain
   is concentrated in a subset of simulations rather than being a uniform shift. Quote
   the paired mean difference with its CI, not the difference of medians.

5. **Scenario-level numbers are sweep-composition dependent.** Each scenario averages over
   a difficulty sweep, so a scenario median or paired mean shifts if the sweep's parameter
   grid changes, without anything about the methods changing. Quote the per-sweep-point
   breakdown (§2a, `synthetic_sweep_paired.parquet`) alongside any scenario-level figure,
   and never describe a scenario median as evidence of robustness across that scenario's
   parameter range — at the hardest point of three of the five headline scenarios the
   IsoGraph-vs-WGCNA advantage is not demonstrable.
